## Key claim

**An anomaly can exclude a trivially gapped confined phase without deciding how deconfinement occurs.**

It may order two transition temperatures while leaving their separation, transition orders, and critical exponents undetermined.

## Theme

**The CP–center anomaly as an ordering constraint on thermal transitions.**

## Guiding question

How can a four-dimensional anomaly involving a one-form symmetry constrain the ordinary $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry of a Polyakov loop?

For pure $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Yang–Mills at $\theta=\pi$, CP and the electric $\mathbb Z_2^{(1)}$ center symmetry have a mixed 't Hooft anomaly.

Thermal compactification splits the four-dimensional center background into two pieces. One still probes spatial Wilson loops. The other becomes the background for the zero-form center symmetry acting on the Polyakov loop.

The anomaly survives this split. Its consequences for phases, however, require infrared assumptions.

## Setup

Put pure $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Yang–Mills on

$$
M_4=S^1_\beta\times M_3,
\qquad
\beta=T^{-1},
$$

with Euclidean action

$$
S_E
=
\frac{1}{2g^2}
\int_{M_4}\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge *F)
-\ii\theta Q,
\qquad
Q
=
\frac{1}{8\pi^2}
\int_{M_4}\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F).
$$

The trace convention is chosen so that $Q\in\mathbb Z$ for an ordinary $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ bundle.

At $\theta=\pi$, CP sends

$$
\theta\longmapsto-\theta=-\pi,
$$

which returns to $\pi$ after a $2\pi$ shift. Without a center background, this makes CP a symmetry.

Now couple the electric one-form center symmetry to

$$
B\in H^2(M_4,\mathbb Z_2).
$$

In this background, the instanton number can become fractional. The $2\pi$ shift needed by CP is no longer invisible.

In a standard choice of local counterterm, the anomalous transformation is represented by

$$
Z_\pi[B]
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CP}}
Z_\pi[B]\,
\exp\left[
\frac{\ii\pi}{2}
\int_{M_4}\mathcal P(B)
\right],
$$

where

$$
\mathcal P:
H^2(M_4,\mathbb Z_2)
\longrightarrow
H^4(M_4,\mathbb Z_4)
$$

is the Pontryagin square.

Changing the counterterm convention may change the representative phase. It cannot remove the mixed anomaly while preserving the required symmetries.

This transformation law is the exact ultraviolet statement. It contains no claim yet about a mass gap, spontaneous symmetry breaking, or a transition temperature.

## Analysis

### Derivation: reduce the anomaly

Let

$$
u\in H^1(S^1_\beta,\mathbb Z_2),
\qquad
\int_{S^1_\beta}u=1.
$$

For backgrounds pulled back from $M_3$, decompose

$$
B=B_s+A\smile u,
$$

with

$$
B_s\in H^2(M_3,\mathbb Z_2),
\qquad
A\in H^1(M_3,\mathbb Z_2).
$$

The two fields have different three-dimensional jobs:

| Background | Three-dimensional symmetry | Charged probe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $B_s$ | Spatial $\mathbb Z_2^{(1)}$ center | Spatial fundamental Wilson loop |
| $A$ | Thermal $\mathbb Z_2^{(0)}$ center | Fundamental Polyakov loop |

Write the Polyakov loop using $\mathcal A$ for the dynamical Yang–Mills connection:

$$
P(\mathbf x)
=
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbf 2}
\mathcal P
\exp\left[
\ii\int_0^\beta
\mathcal A_0(\tau,\mathbf x)\,\dd\tau
\right].
$$

The thermal center acts as

$$
P(\mathbf x)\longmapsto-P(\mathbf x).
$$

The Pontryagin square is a quadratic refinement:

$$
\mathcal P(x+y)
=
\mathcal P(x)
+\mathcal P(y)
+2x\smile y
\pmod 4.
$$

Therefore the part of $\mathcal P(B)$ that depends on both $A$ and $B_s$ is

$$
2B_s\smile A\smile u.
$$

Integrating over the thermal circle gives

$$
\frac12
\int_{S^1_\beta\times M_3}
2B_s\smile A\smile u
=
\int_{M_3}A\smile B_s
\pmod 2.
$$

The four-dimensional anomaly thus contains the three-dimensional response

$$
\boxed{
Z[A,B_s]
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CP}}
Z[A,B_s]\,
(-1)^{\int_{M_3}A\smile B_s}
}.
$$

A one-form symmetry has not turned wholesale into a zero-form symmetry. Its background has split into a spatial two-form field and a one-form field obtained by placing one leg on the thermal circle.

### Scope and assumptions: isolate the infrared assumptions

Anomaly matching forbids a unique, symmetry-preserving, trivially gapped infrared phase.

To translate that statement into an ordering of thermal transitions, assume that the relevant three-dimensional thermal regime has:

1. locality and a mass gap;
2. unbroken spatial $\mathbb Z_2^{(1)}$ center symmetry;
3. no intrinsic topological order or other nontrivial long-range sector.

In a center-symmetric confined phase,

$$
\langle P\rangle=0,
$$

so the thermal $\mathbb Z_2^{(0)}$ center is unbroken.

Suppose CP restored at a lower temperature than deconfinement:

$$
T_{\mathrm{CP}}<T_{\mathrm{deconf}}.
$$

Then the interval

$$
T_{\mathrm{CP}}<T<T_{\mathrm{deconf}}
$$

would preserve CP, thermal center, and, by assumption, spatial center.

If that interval were also gapped and topologically trivial, all anomalous symmetries would be preserved in a forbidden infrared phase. Hence

$$
\boxed{
T_{\mathrm{deconf}}
\leq
T_{\mathrm{CP}}
}
$$

under the stated assumptions.

The loopholes are physical, not semantic. A gapless phase, intrinsic topological order, or broken spatial center can match the anomaly without the conventional ordering argument.

### Physical interpretation: what the inequality does and does not know

The reduced anomaly connects generalized symmetry to a Landau order parameter:

$$
\mathbb Z_2^{(1)}
\quad\xrightarrow{\text{thermal split}}\quad
\mathbb Z_2^{(1)}{}_{\rm spatial}
\times
\mathbb Z_2^{(0)}{}_{\rm thermal}.
$$

This explains why the Polyakov loop enters an argument that began with a one-form symmetry.

It does not turn the Yang–Mills theory into a unique three-dimensional Ising model. A potential $V(P)$ can describe thermal-center breaking, but $P$ alone cannot reproduce the response

$$
(-1)^{\int A\smile B_s}.
$$

The spatial one-form sector must remain visible in any effective description that claims to match the full anomaly.

The inequality also allows a deconfined but CP-broken window:

$$
T_{\mathrm{deconf}}
<
T
<
T_{\mathrm{CP}}.
$$

In that phase, thermal center is broken, so the anomaly is already matched even though CP remains broken.

Recent lattice work using analytic continuation from imaginary $\theta$ reports evidence for precisely this strict ordering in pure $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Yang–Mills. That is dynamical evidence, not part of the anomaly theorem.

## False claim to diagnose

> Because the anomaly is invariant under renormalization-group flow, deconfinement and CP restoration must occur at the same temperature and share a universality class.

Anomaly matching constrains symmetry realization. It does not select a unique path through the space of phases.

Equality,

$$
T_{\mathrm{deconf}}=T_{\mathrm{CP}},
$$

is allowed but not required. A strict inequality is also allowed, as is a first-order transition. If a continuous transition occurs, its critical exponents require further dynamical information.

A defensible replacement is:

> Given the infrared assumptions above, CP cannot be restored inside a center-symmetric confined phase.

## What follows — and what does not

Keep the logical layers separate:

| Claim | Status |
| --- | --- |
| CP at $\theta=\pi$ has a mixed anomaly with $\mathbb Z_2^{(1)}$ center. | Exact symmetry statement |
| Thermal compactification yields a response $(-1)^{\int A\smile B_s}$. | Exact background-field reduction |
| A unique, symmetric, trivially gapped phase is impossible. | Anomaly-matching consequence |
| $T_{\mathrm{deconf}}\leq T_{\mathrm{CP}}$. | Consequence with explicit infrared assumptions |
| The two transitions coincide or share critical exponents. | Not fixed by the anomaly |
| Pure $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ has a CP-broken deconfined window. | Dynamical question with recent numerical evidence |

The anomaly removes regions from the phase diagram. It does not draw the remaining phase boundaries for us.

## Exercise

On $S^1_\beta\times M_3$, take

$$
B=B_s+A\smile u,
\qquad
\int_{S^1_\beta}u=1,
$$

and use

$$
\mathcal P(x+y)
=
\mathcal P(x)+\mathcal P(y)+2x\smile y
\pmod 4.

1. Extract the part of

   $$
   \frac12
   \int_{S^1_\beta\times M_3}

   $$

2. Show that CP produces

   $$
   (-1)^{\int_{M_3}A\smile B_s}.
   $$

3. Assume a gap, unbroken spatial center, and no intrinsic topological order. Prove by contradiction that

   $$
   T_{\mathrm{CP}}<T_{\mathrm{deconf}}
   $$

   is impossible.

<details class="solution">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The quadratic property gives the mixed term

$$
2B_s\smile A\smile u.
$$

Thus

$$
\frac12
\int_{S^1_\beta\times M_3}
2B_s\smile A\smile u
=
\int_{M_3}A\smile B_s
\pmod 2.
$$

Substitution into the CP phase yields

$$
\exp\left[
\ii\pi
\int_{M_3}A\smile B_s
\right]
=
(-1)^{\int_{M_3}A\smile B_s}.
$$

Now suppose

$$
T_{\mathrm{CP}}<T_{\mathrm{deconf}}.
$$

Immediately above $T_{\mathrm{CP}}$ but below $T_{\mathrm{deconf}}$, CP is restored while thermal center remains unbroken.

If spatial center also remains unbroken, the phase preserves all three symmetries appearing in the reduced anomaly.

A local, gapped, topologically trivial phase cannot do so. Under these assumptions, the proposed interval is impossible, and therefore

$$
T_{\mathrm{deconf}}\leq T_{\mathrm{CP}}.
$$

No contradiction arises when the inequality is strict in the opposite direction. Between $T_{\mathrm{deconf}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{CP}}$, thermal center is broken and can match the anomaly.

</details>
