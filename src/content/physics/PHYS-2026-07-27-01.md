## Today's Fracture

**A many-body path integral is not fundamentally a movie of particles following trajectories.**

Its integration variables may be coherent-state amplitudes, Grassmann fields, order parameters, gauge fields, noise histories, replicas, or auxiliary fields that were absent from the original Hamiltonian.

An instanton is one possible saddle of that functional integral. It is not the definition of the method.

## Theme

**From exact representations to collective fields: what many-body path integrals do before, around, and beyond instantons.**

## Core Provocation

For an interacting quantum system, when is a path integral:

1. an exact rewriting of the operator problem;
2. a controlled collective-field approximation;
3. a numerical sampling problem;
4. a real-time interference problem;
5. a search for nonperturbative saddles?

The central skill is to identify which layer a statement belongs to.

## Minimal Setup — one model, several path integrals

Take the attractive two-site Bose–Hubbard model at fixed total particle number $N$:

$$
H
=-\frac K2
\left(
a_L^\dagger a_R+a_R^\dagger a_L
\right)
-\frac g2
\sum_{\alpha=L,R}
n_\alpha(n_\alpha-1),
\qquad g>0.
$$

The equilibrium partition function begins as an operator identity:

$$
Z=\Tr\ee^{-\beta H}.
$$

Insert coherent-state resolutions of the identity between short imaginary-time steps. In the continuum notation this becomes

$$
Z
=\int_{\rm periodic}
\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\,
\ee^{-S_E[\bar\psi,\psi]}.
$$

The time lattice, measure, boundary conditions, and operator ordering are part of the definition. The continuum expression is shorthand for their controlled limit.

At fixed $N$, introduce the population imbalance and relative phase,

$$
z=\frac{n_L-n_R}{N},
\qquad
\phi=\phi_L-\phi_R.
$$

On the spin-coherent-state sphere, the classical Hamiltonian is, up to a constant,

$$
H_{\rm cl}(z,\phi)
=-\frac{KN}{2}
\sqrt{1-z^2}\cos\phi
-\frac{gN^2}{4}z^2.
$$

Define

$$
\Lambda=\frac{gN}{K}.
$$

For $\Lambda>1$, mean-field theory has two degenerate minima,

$$
z=\pm z_0,
\qquad
z_0=\sqrt{1-\Lambda^{-2}},
\qquad
\phi=0.
$$

The collective Euclidean action is

$$
S_E[z,\phi]
=\int\dd\tau
\left[
\frac{\ii N}{2}(1-z)\dot\phi
+H_{\rm cl}(z,\phi)
\right],
$$

up to a Berry-phase gauge choice and boundary terms.

This model will anchor the instanton calculation. But the broader path-integral machinery begins earlier and extends much further.

## The Path-Integral Ladder

### Level 1 — exact representation

Trotter decomposition and coherent-state insertion rewrite an operator trace or amplitude as a sum over histories.

No saddle point has yet been assumed.

### Level 2 — exact change or enlargement of variables

A Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation introduces an auxiliary field to linearize an interaction.

The new field need not be a microscopic observable.

### Level 3 — controlled or uncontrolled truncation

A stationary auxiliary field gives mean-field theory. Its Hessian gives Gaussian fluctuations, collective modes, and often RPA-type response.

The approximation is controlled only when a small parameter, large-$N$ limit, weak coupling, or separation of scales has been established.

### Level 4 — nonperturbative sectors

Instantons, vortices, skyrmions, bounces, and complex saddles capture contributions invisible to expansion around one vacuum.

Which saddle contributes depends on boundary conditions and the integration contour, not merely on stationary equations.

### Level 5 — computational representation

The discretized functional integral may be sampled by Monte Carlo, reorganized diagrammatically, deformed into complex space, or contracted as a tensor network.

An exact formal integral can still be exponentially difficult to evaluate.

## Three Lenses

### Builder — interactions become fluctuating fields

Consider a schematic fermionic action

$$
S[\bar\psi,\psi]
=
\bar\psi G_0^{-1}\psi
+\frac U2\int_x
\mathcal O(x)^2.
$$

A continuous Hubbard–Stratonovich identity has the form

$$
\exp\left[
-\frac U2\mathcal O^2
\right]
\propto
\int\dd\phi\,
\exp\left[
-\frac{\phi^2}{2U}
+\ii\phi\mathcal O
\right],
$$

with contours and factors adjusted to the sign of $U$ and the chosen channel.

The fermions are now quadratic and can be integrated out:

$$
Z
=\int\mathcal D\phi\,
\ee^{-S_{\rm eff}[\phi]},
$$

where

$$
S_{\rm eff}[\phi]
=
\frac{1}{2U}\int_x\phi(x)^2
-\Tr\log
\left[
G_0^{-1}+\ii\Gamma\phi
\right].
$$

This single equation contains several familiar theories.

The saddle equation

$$
\frac{\delta S_{\rm eff}}{\delta\phi(x)}=0
$$

is a self-consistent mean-field or gap equation.

Expanding

$$
\phi=\phi_\star+\delta\phi
$$

gives

$$
S_{\rm eff}
=S_{\rm eff}[\phi_\star]
+\frac12
\delta\phi\,\Gamma^{(2)}\,\delta\phi
+O(\delta\phi^3).
$$

Zeros or poles of the inverse fluctuation kernel $\Gamma^{(2)}$ determine collective modes and instabilities.

The field $\phi$ can represent density, magnetization, pairing, or another channel. Exact HS representations can be equivalent, while their saddle truncations are not.

This is the practical content of the Fierz ambiguity: changing decoupling channel can change an approximate answer even when the untruncated integral is unchanged.

### Skeptic — a functional integral is not automatically a probability measure

After fermions are integrated out, auxiliary-field Monte Carlo often produces

$$
Z
=\int\mathcal D\phi\,
\ee^{-S_B[\phi]}
\det M_\uparrow[\phi]
\det M_\downarrow[\phi].
$$

In special symmetry classes, the determinant product is nonnegative. The half-filled repulsive Hubbard model on a bipartite lattice is a standard example.

Away from such conditions, the weight may change sign or acquire a complex phase.

Sampling the absolute value gives

$$
\langle\ee^{\ii\theta}\rangle_{|w|}
=\frac{Z}{Z_{|w|}}
\sim
\ee^{-\beta V\Delta f}.
$$

If $\Delta f>0$, the signal-to-noise ratio decays exponentially with inverse temperature $\beta$ and volume $V$.

The generic sign problem is NP-hard. This does **not** mean every sign problem is equally hard, or that symmetry cannot remove it in important model classes.

Three further cautions matter.

First, a Trotterized action has discretization error until the time-step limit is taken or a continuous-time formulation is used.

Second, coherent-state actions contain Berry phases and endpoint subtleties. Naively replacing operators by commuting numbers can miss ordering corrections.

Third, near a critical point the Hessian develops soft directions. A Gaussian expansion can fail precisely where the collective fluctuations become most interesting.

The existence of $S_{\rm eff}$ is exact. The dominance of one smooth, real saddle is a separate hypothesis.

### Translator — choose the contour to match the question

Imaginary time is designed for equilibrium:

$$
Z=\Tr\ee^{-\beta H}
=\int\mathcal D\phi\,
\ee^{-S_E[\phi]}.
$$

It gives thermodynamics, Matsubara correlators, and ground-state projection.

Real-time response and nonequilibrium evolution require a forward-and-backward Schwinger–Keldysh contour:

$$
Z
=\int
\mathcal D\phi_+\,
\mathcal D\phi_-\,
\ee^{
\ii S[\phi_+]
-\ii S[\phi_-]
}.
$$

The two copies are not redundant. They encode the evolution of the ket and bra and enforce normalization and causality.

The Keldysh rotation

$$
\phi_{\rm cl}
=\frac{\phi_++\phi_-}{2},
\qquad
\phi_{\rm q}
=\phi_+-\phi_-
$$

separates classical histories from response or quantum components.

After an environment is integrated out, the reduced system acquires an influence action:

$$
Z_{\rm red}
=\int
\mathcal D\phi_+\,
\mathcal D\phi_-\,
\ee^{
\ii S[\phi_+]
-\ii S[\phi_-]
+\ii S_{\rm IF}[\phi_+,\phi_-]
}.
$$

The influence functional is generally nonlocal in time. Its retarded part encodes dissipation; its Keldysh component encodes fluctuations and noise.

A stochastic weak-noise process can also have

$$
P[x]\asymp
\ee^{-I[x]/\epsilon}.
$$

Its optimal rare path and a Euclidean instanton share saddle-point mathematics.

The ontology differs. A stochastic path carries probability, while a quantum path contributes a complex amplitude and can interfere destructively with other saddles.

## The Instanton Sector — one nonperturbative use

Return to the attractive Bose–Hubbard dimer.

The two mean-field minima are approximately

$$
\ket{N,0},
\qquad
\ket{0,N}.
$$

A collective saddle connects $z=+z_0$ to $z=-z_0$ in Euclidean time.

Because the action is first order and contains a Berry phase, its saddle equations are imaginary-time Hamilton equations.

They are not Newton’s equation for a particle in an inverted potential.

The phase $\phi$ generally becomes complex along the connecting saddle. Eliminating it can produce a one-coordinate action for $z$, but with a coordinate-dependent kinetic term.

At large $N$,

$$
\Delta E
\sim
A(N,\Lambda)
\ee^{-Nb(\Lambda)}.
$$

Here

$$
\hbar_{\rm eff}\sim\frac1N.
$$

The factor $N$ is collective semiclassical scaling. It does not mean that $N$ independent particles each pay the same tunnelling action.

### The microscopic Fock-space picture

The hopping operator transfers only one boson:

$$
\ket{N-k+1,k-1}
\longrightarrow
\ket{N-k,k}.
$$

The endpoint states first couple at order $N$ in $K$.

For the unperturbed interaction Hamiltonian,

$$
E_k-E_0=gk(N-k).
$$

The virtual states near $k=N/2$ are the most energetically expensive.

Their chain of matrix elements and denominators is the microscopic Fock-space structure reorganized by the collective instanton.

### When the one-instanton picture fails

Near $\Lambda=1$, a collective mode softens. The fluctuation determinant becomes singular, so the standard dilute-instanton expansion is not uniform.

Coupling to a gapless environment may generate a memory kernel:

$$
S_{\rm diss}
\sim
\int\dd\tau\,\dd\tau'\,
\frac{
[z(\tau)-z(\tau')]^2
}{
(\tau-\tau')^2
}.
$$

The environment can change the tunnelling exponent, destroy coherence, or produce a localization transition.

Multiple complex saddles can also interfere. Solving the saddle equations is insufficient; one must determine which saddles lie on contours connected to the original integral.

## Frontier Map — beyond one saddle

### 1. Complex contours and Lefschetz thimbles

Holomorphic flow deforms the original integration manifold into complex field space without crossing singularities.

Near a thimble, phase fluctuations can be reduced. Hubbard-model studies have shown substantial improvement in regimes where the undeformed average sign is exponentially small.

The method is not a universal cure. Residual phases, Jacobians, multimodal sampling, singularities, and interference among several thimbles can remain decisive.

### 2. Symmetry-designed sign-free formulations

Some auxiliary-field decompositions produce determinant pairs related by antiunitary symmetry:

$$
\det M_2[\phi]
=
\det M_1[\phi]^*.
$$

Then

$$
\det M_1\det M_2
=
|\det M_1|^2
\geq0.
$$

This is an exact escape from the sign problem for a class of models, not an approximation.

The active frontier is to enlarge those classes through Majorana representations, symmetry criteria, basis changes, and redesigned interactions.

### 3. Generative and flow-based sampling

Normalizing flows learn an invertible map from a simple reference distribution to field configurations.

When combined with reweighting or a Metropolis correction, proposals can remain asymptotically exact.

Gauge-equivariant flows can build local symmetry into the architecture. Proof-of-principle work has reached four-dimensional $SU(3)$ lattice gauge theory.

Training cost, mode coverage, volume scaling, topology freezing, and transfer between couplings remain open limitations.

A learned sampler accelerates exploration of the measure. It does not change the theory’s partition function or automatically solve a complex-phase problem.

### 4. Temporal tensor networks

A discretized path integral is a tensor network in spacetime.

For open systems, the influence functional can be represented as a matrix product operator along the time direction.

TEMPO compresses bath memory when temporal correlations have manageable bond dimension.

Recent extensions treat Grassmann influence functionals for fermionic impurity models. 2026 preprints extend the framework to more general bosonic couplings and superconducting fermionic baths.

The bottleneck becomes temporal entanglement or operator-space complexity. Long memory and strong nonequilibrium correlations can still force rapid bond-dimension growth.

This is a conceptual reversal:

> Instead of sampling all histories, compress the correlations among histories.

## Thought Striker — deliberately false

> Once an interacting system has been written as a path integral, the many-body problem has been solved; all that remains is approximation detail.

The formal rewriting can be exact while the evaluation remains exponentially hard.

The action may be complex, nonlocal, multimodal, singular, or dominated by several interfering sectors.

Even the choice of variables can move difficulty between the action, measure, contour, and observable.

The defensible statement is:

> A path integral exposes new organizations of the many-body problem—by fields, topology, saddles, stochastic samples, or tensor contractions—but does not guarantee that any organization is efficient.

## Collision

The apparent disagreements are resolved by keeping five layers separate:

| Layer | Object | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operator theory | $\Tr\ee^{-\beta H}$ or a real-time amplitude | Exact starting problem |
| Functional representation | Coherent, Grassmann, auxiliary, or contour fields | Exact when the measure and regulator are specified |
| Effective description | Saddle, gradient expansion, or low-energy field | Controlled only with a scale argument |
| Nonperturbative sector | Instantons, defects, complex saddles | Contribution depends on contour and boundary data |
| Numerical realization | Monte Carlo, flows, diagrams, tensor contraction | Accuracy and cost are method-dependent |

The many-body instanton belongs to the fourth row.

It should not eclipse the other four.

## Exercise — recover the instanton exponent microscopically

Work in the strong-attraction regime

$$
gN\gg K.
$$

Starting from $\ket{N,0}$, compute the leading off-diagonal matrix element connecting it to $\ket{0,N}$ using $N$th-order degenerate perturbation theory.

Show that

$$
\boxed{
\Delta_N
=
2
\frac{
(K/2)^N N!
}{
g^{N-1}[(N-1)!]^2
}
}.
$$

At fixed

$$
\Lambda=\frac{gN}{K}\gg1,
$$

use Stirling’s approximation to extract the leading exponential behavior.

Then answer the transfer question:

> Which part of this result would be missed by keeping only one Gaussian expansion around either minimum?

**Oral check 1.** Why does the coupling first appear at order $N$, even though the Hamiltonian is only quadratic in creation and annihilation operators?

**Oral check 2.** In auxiliary-field Monte Carlo, are the sampled variables electron trajectories, microscopic observables, or decoupling fields?

**Oral check 3.** Why can a Keldysh path integral not be interpreted as an ordinary probability distribution over histories?

<details class="solution">
<summary>Solution</summary>

There is one minimal monotonic sequence:

$$
\ket{N,0}
\to
\ket{N-1,1}
\to\cdots\to
\ket{0,N}.
$$

The product of hopping matrix elements is

$$
\prod_{k=1}^{N}
\left[
-\frac K2
\sqrt{k(N-k+1)}
\right]
=
\left(-\frac K2\right)^N N!.
$$

The $N-1$ intermediate denominators are

$$
E_0-E_k=-gk(N-k).
$$

Therefore,

$$
|t_{\rm eff}|
=
\frac{
(K/2)^N N!
}{
g^{N-1}[(N-1)!]^2
}.
$$

The symmetric and antisymmetric combinations split by

$$
\Delta_N=2|t_{\rm eff}|.
$$

Substitute

$$
g=\frac{\Lambda K}{N}.
$$

Then

$$
\Delta_N
=
K\,2^{1-N}
\frac{
N^N
}{
\Lambda^{N-1}(N-1)!
}.
$$

Stirling’s formula gives

$$
\boxed{
\log\Delta_N
=
-N[\log(2\Lambda)-1]
+O(\log N)
}.
$$

Thus

$$
\Delta_N
\sim
\operatorname{poly}(N)
\exp\left\{
-N[\log(2\Lambda)-1]
\right\}.
$$

The exponent is the strong-coupling limit of the collective instanton action.

The algebraic corrections belong to the prefactor and to finite-$N$ matching.

A Gaussian expansion around one minimum describes perturbative fluctuations that remain in that basin.

It cannot generate the exponentially small matrix element connecting two distinct saddles. That contribution is nonperturbative in the local expansion.

The auxiliary-field variables in Monte Carlo are decoupling fields. Their relation to observables depends on the chosen channel and source derivatives.

A Keldysh weight is complex:

$$
\ee^{\ii S[\phi_+]-\ii S[\phi_-]}.
$$

It sums amplitudes with interference and therefore is not an ordinary positive history probability.

</details>

## Exit Ticket

Take one interacting problem you know and identify:

1. the exact path-integral variables;
2. any auxiliary or collective fields;
3. the small parameter behind a saddle expansion;
4. the main numerical obstruction;
5. whether the relevant contour is Euclidean, Keldysh, or complex-deformed.

If you cannot name item 3, you do not yet know why the approximation should work.

You may also reply with “deeper,” “too easy,” “too hard,” or your perturbative derivation.

## Continuity Ledger

- Pressure point: a path integral is a representation, not an automatic solution.
- Exact layer: Trotterization, coherent states, and Hubbard–Stratonovich identities.
- Approximation layer: mean field, Gaussian fluctuations, and collective effective actions.
- Nonperturbative layer: instantons, defects, Berry phases, and complex saddles.
- Computational layer: sign-free symmetry, contour deformation, generative sampling, and temporal tensor networks.
