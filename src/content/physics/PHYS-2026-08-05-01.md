## Key claim

**An internal-state synthetic dimension can reproduce a Hofstadter hopping graph and its gauge-invariant flux exactly within the single-particle model. The same identification need not preserve locality once particles interact.**

Atoms in distant internal states can occupy the same real-space Wannier orbital. Contact collisions may then couple every pair of synthetic sites in a column with comparable strength.

The kinetic graph and the interaction metric are separate pieces of a many-body Hamiltonian. A faithful simulation of the first does not supply the second.

## Theme

**Gauge-equivalent hopping geometry and interaction locality in synthetic dimensions.**

## Guiding question

When does the map

$$
\ket{n,m}\longleftrightarrow\ket{x=n,y=m}
$$

justify treating an internal state $m$ as a spatial coordinate? Which many-body conclusions require more than this map?

## Setup

Take a one-dimensional optical lattice with sites $n$ and spacing $a$. Each atom has $W=2F+1$ internal states

$$
m=-F,-F+1,\ldots,F.
$$

Ordinary tunnelling moves an atom along $n$. Raman transitions move it between neighboring internal states and imprint a position-dependent phase. A representative tight-binding Hamiltonian is

$$
H_0=\sum_{n,m}
\left[
-t\,a_{n+1,m}^{\dagger}a_{n,m}
+\Omega_m\ee^{-\ii\gamma n}
a_{n,m+1}^{\dagger}a_{n,m}
+\mathrm{H.c.}
\right],
$$

with

$$
\gamma=2k_Ra
$$

for the Raman momentum transfer used in the simplest construction. The absence of couplings beyond $m=\pm F$ gives open boundaries in the synthetic direction.

For approximately $SU(W)$-symmetric bosonic contact interactions, projection into the lowest Wannier orbital gives

$$
H_{\rm int}
=\frac{U}{2}\sum_nN_n(N_n-1),
\qquad
N_n=\sum_m n_{n,m}.
$$

The Hamiltonian is local in the physical coordinate $n$. Its range in the coordinate $m$ will be the central issue.

> [!margin: Platform matters]
> The locality problem discussed here concerns internal-state synthetic dimensions with contact collisions. Frequency modes, momentum lattices, photonic systems, and engineered state-dependent interactions can realize different kernels.

## Analysis

### 1. The plaquette flux is an exact graph statement

Ignore the common real hopping amplitude and assign oriented link variables

$$
U_x(n,m)=1,
\qquad
U_m(n,m)=\ee^{-\ii\gamma n}.
$$

Traverse the plaquette

$$
(n,m)\to(n+1,m)\to(n+1,m+1)
\to(n,m+1)\to(n,m).
$$

The ordered product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_\square
&=U_x(n,m)U_m(n+1,m)
U_x^\dagger(n,m+1)U_m^\dagger(n,m)\\
&=\ee^{-\ii\gamma(n+1)}\ee^{+\ii\gamma n}\\
&=\boxed{\ee^{-\ii\gamma}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The sign changes if the loop orientation is reversed. The signed flux in the convention above is

$$
\frac{\Phi_\square}{\Phi_0}=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}
\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 1).
$$

Under a local basis change

$$
a_{n,m}\mapsto\ee^{\ii\chi_{n,m}}a_{n,m},
$$

each link gains endpoint phases. They cancel around a closed loop, leaving $W_\square$ invariant.

This establishes a uniform Abelian lattice flux. Within the tight-binding Hilbert space, the synthetic system and a charged particle on the corresponding Hofstadter strip have the same one-body connectivity and link phases.

Celi and collaborators used this construction to predict Hofstadter spectra and edge modes localized near $m=\pm F$. Subsequent cold-atom experiments observed chiral dynamics in synthetic Hall ribbons.

> [!margin: What “exact” means]
> The graph equivalence holds for the modeled one-body Hamiltonian. Raman detunings, unequal $\Omega_m$, additional atomic levels, heating, and finite strip width remain experimental corrections.

A wider internal-state manifold separates the two synthetic edges and supplies more bulk-like orbitals. It does not automatically reproduce a two-dimensional thermodynamic limit: the range of $m$, boundary couplings, and interaction scaling must also be specified.

### 2. Contact locality becomes all-to-all synthetic coupling

Expand the $SU(W)$-symmetric interaction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\rm int}
=\frac{U}{2}\sum_n
\bigg[
&\sum_m n_{n,m}(n_{n,m}-1)\\
&+2\sum_{m<m'}n_{n,m}n_{n,m'}
\bigg].
\end{aligned}
$$

Two atoms in different internal states $m$ and $m'$ interact with strength $U$ whenever they occupy the same real-space site $n$. That strength is independent of

$$
d_m=|m-m'|.
$$

In the synthetic strip metric, the density interaction kernel is therefore

$$
V(\Delta n,\Delta m)
=U\,\delta_{\Delta n,0}
\quad\text{for }\Delta m\neq0,
$$

up to species-dependent scattering lengths and the same-state term. Its range along $m$ grows with $W$.

A short-range two-dimensional interaction would instead obey a decay condition such as

$$
|V(\Delta n,\Delta m)|
\longrightarrow0
$$

as the two-dimensional separation becomes large. Power-law interactions require their own decay bounds; an all-to-all column interaction lies outside the usual short-range case.

The mismatch can be stated without metaphor:

| Structure | Distance it uses | Result in the internal-state construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-body hopping | Graph distance on $(n,m)$ | Nearest-neighbor Hofstadter strip |
| Contact collision | Physical overlap of atomic wavefunctions | Local in $n$, generically long-range in $m$ |
| Edge | Termination of the hopping graph | Physical absence of further coupled internal states |
| Many-body locality | Decay of interaction terms in a chosen metric | Not supplied by the hopping graph |

The statistics of the atoms add another layer. Bosonic and fermionic particles can occupy the same hopping graph but have different on-site constraints and exchange signs. Single-particle graph equivalence does not determine those data either.

### 3. Why locality enters topological phase equivalence

For short-range lattice Hamiltonians, a uniformly gapped path

$$
H(s),\qquad 0\leq s\leq1,
$$

supports quasi-adiabatic continuation. Under the required locality and gap assumptions, one constructs a quasi-local unitary that transports the ground-state subspace along the path.

This is the basis for treating two gapped Hamiltonians as representatives of the same many-body phase. Lieb–Robinson bounds and related stability results control how rapidly the continuation spreads information.

An interpolation containing interactions of unbounded range in the synthetic metric does not satisfy the standard short-range hypothesis. A nonclosing gap along such a path can connect states that no finite-depth or quasi-local two-dimensional evolution could connect.

The conclusion is limited but important:

$$
\text{gapped nonlocal path}
\centernot\Longrightarrow
\text{same local 2D phase}.
$$

Nor does one quantized response invariant repair the missing hypothesis. A many-body Chern number is defined through twisted boundary conditions and can remain quantized in settings where the interaction metric is nonlocal. It does not, by itself, certify robustness against every local perturbation or establish the full pattern of long-range entanglement.

> [!margin: Gap plus locality]
> “The gap did not close” has phase-classification force only after the allowed Hamiltonian path and its locality class have been fixed.

A 2026 preprint by Geraghty, Nardin, Mazza, and Rizzi studies an extended Harper–Hofstadter model with an infinite-range interaction within each synthetic column. In their finite-size calculations, increasing that interaction connects a bosonic Laughlin-type fractional Chern regime to a momentum-space Tao–Thouless-like charge-density wave without closing the many-body gap.

The computed many-body Chern number and fitted topological entanglement entropy remain approximately unchanged, while the particle entanglement spectrum restructures and robustness to a local perturbation is lost. The authors interpret the large-interaction regime as lacking genuine locality-protected topological order.

This is evidence from a recent numerical preprint, not a general theorem. Its scope depends on the model, finite-size scaling, the definition of locality, and the diagnostics used. It nevertheless supplies a concrete counterexample to the inference that a preserved gap and Chern number alone establish equivalence as local two-dimensional phases.

### 4. Gauge algebra and interaction locality are independent

Synthetic links can also be matrix-valued:

$$
U_{ij}\in SU(2).
$$

For a closed path $C$, the ordered product is

$$
W_C=\mathcal P\prod_{(ij)\in C}U_{ij}.
$$

Under a local non-Abelian gauge transformation based at $i_0$,

$$
W_C\mapsto G_{i_0}W_CG_{i_0}^{-1}.
$$

Thus the Wilson-loop matrix is gauge covariant. Its trace, determinant, and eigenvalues are gauge invariant. Experiments have realized tunable synthetic $SU(2)$ fields and measured their non-Abelian effect on chiral dynamics.

Changing scalar links into matrix-valued links enriches the one-body parallel transport. It does not alter the range of a density interaction unless the interaction engineering changes as well.

The two design questions are independent:

1. Which connection governs hopping and interference?
2. Which metric controls the decay of many-body couplings?

Schemes based on state-dependent spatial separation, Rydberg dressing, cavity mediation, or fast interaction Trotterization can reshape the second answer. Such engineering is an additional construction, not a consequence of synthetic gauge flux.

## False claim to diagnose

> If an interacting synthetic-dimension system remains gapped and its many-body Chern number stays quantized during a deformation, it must remain in the same two-dimensional topological phase.

The inference omits the class of allowed deformations. Standard adiabatic phase equivalence restricts the path to Hamiltonians that remain sufficiently local in the physical metric used to define the phase.

A gap and a Chern number remain valuable diagnostics. They do not show that a nonlocal path can be replaced by a quasi-local one.

## Exercise

Close the synthetic direction into a ring with $W$ states. Let the density interaction at each physical site have kernel

$$
V(d)=
\begin{cases}
0,&d=0,\\
U,&d=1,\ldots,W-1,
\end{cases}
$$

where $d$ is understood modulo $W$.

1. Recompute the plaquette Wilson loop and show explicitly that local $U(1)$ endpoint phases cancel.
2. Evaluate

   $$
   \widetilde V(k)
   =\sum_{d=0}^{W-1}V(d)\ee^{-\ii kd},
   \qquad
   k=\frac{2\pi l}{W}.
   $$

3. Compare the result with a nearest-neighbor synthetic interaction

   $$
   V_{\rm nn}(d)=U(\delta_{d,1}+\delta_{d,W-1}).
   $$

4. Decide whether increasing $W$ at fixed $U$ produces a local interacting two-dimensional limit.
5. Explain what changes if one Kac-normalizes the all-to-all coupling as $U/W$. Does that make it short-range?

<summary>Hint 1</summary>

For the allowed momenta,

$$
\sum_{d=0}^{W-1}\ee^{-\ii kd}=W\delta_{k,0}.
$$

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A short-range kernel has a smooth momentum dependence that reflects a finite set of real-space separations. Extensivity and locality are different requirements.

</details>

**Oral check 1.** Can the phase on a single link be gauge invariant?

**Oral check 2.** Which experimental measurement could confirm the Hofstadter hopping graph without testing interaction locality?

<details class="solution">
<summary>Solution</summary>

For the Abelian links, a gauge transformation gives

$$
U_{ij}\mapsto
\ee^{\ii\chi_i}U_{ij}\ee^{-\ii\chi_j}.
$$

Multiplication around the plaquette cancels every phase with the inverse phase from the adjacent link. The result remains

$$
W_\square=\ee^{-\ii\gamma}
$$

for the chosen orientation.

For the all-to-all synthetic kernel,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde V(k)
&=U\sum_{d=1}^{W-1}\ee^{-\ii kd}\\
&=U\left(W\delta_{k,0}-1\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde V(0)=U(W-1),
\qquad
\widetilde V(k\neq0)=-U
}.
$$

The nearest-neighbor kernel instead gives

$$
\widetilde V_{\rm nn}(k)
=U(\ee^{-\ii k}+\ee^{+\ii k})
=2U\cos k.
$$

Its real-space support stays at $d=1$ as $W$ grows. The all-to-all kernel continues to couple sites separated by $O(W)$ with amplitude $U$.

At fixed $U$, the zero-momentum eigenvalue grows as $W$. This warns that a thermodynamic limit must also specify how particle number and coupling scale. Replacing $U$ by $U/W$ makes the collective interaction energy extensive in common large-$W$ limits:

$$
\widetilde V_{\rm Kac}(0)=U\left(1-\frac1W\right),
\qquad
\widetilde V_{\rm Kac}(k\neq0)=-\frac UW.
$$

Kac normalization weakens every pair but leaves the support all-to-all. It restores a form of extensivity; it does not create finite-range locality.

</details>
