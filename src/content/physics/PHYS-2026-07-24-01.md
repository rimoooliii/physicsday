## Today's Fracture

**The comfortable belief under pressure:** the Laughlin state is primarily a clever variational wavefunction whose remarkable properties must be read from its full formula.

What if the decisive datum is instead local?

## Theme

**The Laughlin state is organized by a many-body clustering constraint: local exclusion becomes global density rigidity, and—given a gap and screening—topological order.**

## Core Provocation

Suppose you forgot the explicit Laughlin wavefunction but retained only this statement:

> Whenever two particles approach, the wavefunction vanishes as a prescribed power of their relative coordinate.

How much of the fractional quantum Hall effect could you reconstruct from that local rule?

The answer is substantial but not unlimited. Lowest-Landau-level analyticity turns the zero into exact pseudopotential constraints and flux counting.

Fractional charge, braiding, and topological field theory require additional physical input: a stable gapped phase, screening, and adiabatic continuity.

## Minimal Setup

For fermions at filling $\nu=1/m$, with $m\in2\mathbb{Z}+1$, the Laughlin state on the plane is

$$
\Psi_m(z_1,\ldots,z_N)
=\prod_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^m
\exp\left[-\sum_i\frac{|z_i|^2}{4\ell_B^2}\right].
$$

Its structurally important property is

$$
\Psi_m\sim(z_i-z_j)^m
\qquad\text{as}\qquad z_i\to z_j.
$$

This is a clustering condition on allowed lowest-Landau-level wavefunctions. It fixes their analytic behavior near the particle-coincidence manifold.

The phrase “boundary condition” is useful by analogy, but it must be handled carefully. The condition selects the zero-mode kernel of a parent Hamiltonian; it is not, by itself, the self-adjoint domain of that Hamiltonian.

Compare this with the Bethe–Peierls condition in three dimensions,

$$
\psi(\mathbf r)\sim
\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{a}\right),
\qquad r\to0.
$$

Both replace microscopic interaction details by short-distance asymptotics. Bethe–Peierls regulates a singularity; Laughlin clustering enforces a high-order zero.

## Three Lenses

### Builder — from a pair zero to an exact parent Hamiltonian

In the lowest Landau level, kinetic energy is frozen. Two-particle states can be classified by relative angular momentum $r$.

A rotationally invariant interaction decomposes into Haldane pseudopotentials:

$$
H=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}V_rP_{ij}^{(r)},
$$

where $P_{ij}^{(r)}$ projects particles $i,j$ onto relative angular momentum $r$.

For spin-polarized fermions, only odd $r$ occur. Because

$$
\Psi_m\sim(z_i-z_j)^m,
$$

the Laughlin state has no pair amplitude in the channels

$$
r=1,3,\ldots,m-2.
$$

It is therefore a zero-energy state of the positive-semidefinite parent Hamiltonian

$$
H_{mathrm{parent}}
=\sum_{i<j}
\sum_{\substack{r<m\\r\ \mathrm{odd}}}
V_rP_{ij}^{(r)},
\qquad V_r>0.
$$

For $\nu=1/3$,

$$
H_{mathrm{parent}}=V_1\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^{(1)}.
$$

The state is not merely low in energy. Every positive local projector annihilates it:

$$
P_{ij}^{(r)}\Psi_m=0
\qquad(r<m).
$$

This is the exact chain:

$$
\text{coincidence zero}
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{forbidden pair channels}
\Longrightarrow
\text{frustration-free zero mode}.
$$

### Skeptic — density rigidity is not yet a spectral gap

On the disk, the largest power of each coordinate is

$$
N_{\phi}=m(N-1),
$$

so

$$
\nu=\frac{N}{N_{\phi}}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{m}
$$

in the thermodynamic limit.

Each enforced zero consumes polynomial degree and therefore magnetic flux. At fixed flux, the order-$m$ zero imposes an upper bound on the density.

The Laughlin polynomial is the unique densest zero mode in the standard geometry and sector. This is a strong kinematic rigidity statement.

It does **not** by itself prove a nonzero thermodynamic excitation gap. Calling the state incompressible requires a dynamical statement about the spectrum above the zero-mode sector.

The occupation-number language makes the density constraint visible. The root pattern is

$$
1\,0^{m-1}\,1\,0^{m-1}\,1\cdots,
$$

capturing the $(1,m)$ admissibility rule: no more than one particle appears in any $m$ consecutive orbitals.

This rule organizes zero-mode counting and squeezing patterns. It does not, without further data, determine every coefficient or guarantee a gapped topological phase.

The same warning matters beyond Laughlin states. For the bosonic Moore–Read state, two particles may coincide, but the wavefunction vanishes when three coincide.

The fermionic Moore–Read state still has the pairwise Pauli zero. Its additional defining structure is the three-particle clustering constraint after that compulsory antisymmetry is factored out.

Jain fractions such as

$$
\nu=\frac{n}{2pn\pm1}
$$

are more naturally organized as integer quantum Hall states of composite fermions, not by one simple pairwise vanishing exponent.

### Translator — from an inserted zero to an anyon

A quasihole at $\eta$ is represented by

$$
\Psi_{mathrm{qh}}(\eta)
=\prod_i(z_i-\eta)\Psi_m.
$$

The new factor raises the maximum degree of every electron coordinate by one. Thus it adds one magnetic flux quantum, not $N$ flux quanta, even though the total polynomial degree increases by $N$.

If the bulk is an incompressible screened fluid, adiabatic flux insertion or the plasma argument gives the localized missing electron charge

$$
Q_{mathrm{qh}}=\frac{e}{m},
$$

where the electron has charge $-e$.

Adiabatically taking one quasihole around another produces, up to orientation convention,

$$
\theta_{mathrm{braid}}=\frac{2\pi}{m},
\qquad
\theta_{mathrm{exchange}}=\frac{\pi}{m}.
$$

Three descriptions expose different layers of this result.

#### Plasma analogy

The probability density can be written as

$$
|\Psi_m|^2=\ee^{-\beta U},
\qquad \beta=2m,
$$

with

$$
U=-\sum_{i<j}\log|z_i-z_j|
+\frac{1}{4m\ell_B^2}\sum_i|z_i|^2.
$$

This is a two-dimensional one-component Coulomb plasma. In its screening phase, a quasihole acts as a screened impurity, making its fractional charge local and its long-distance Berry phase well defined.

Screening is physical input. It is not a theorem of holomorphic algebra alone.

#### Conformal field theory

The polynomial factor is a chiral-boson correlator,

$$
\prod_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^m
=\left\langle
\prod_i :\!\ee^{\ii\sqrt{m}\,\phi(z_i)}\!:
\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{bg}}
\right\rangle.
$$

The electron and quasihole vertex operators are

$$
V_e(z)=:\!\ee^{\ii\sqrt{m}\,\phi(z)}\!:,
\qquad
V_{mathrm{qh}}(\eta)=:\!\ee^{\ii\phi(\eta)/\sqrt{m}}\!:.
$$

Their operator-product exponents encode charge and statistics data.

The CFT construction produces conformal-block wavefunctions and candidate edge data. It does not automatically prove that a local bulk Hamiltonian is gapped.

#### Chern–Simons theory

Assuming the Laughlin topological phase, its long-distance response is described by

$$
S[a,A]=\int
\left[
\frac{m}{4\pi}a\wedge\dd a
+\frac{e}{2\pi}A\wedge\dd a
\right].
$$

Integrating out $a$ gives the Hall response magnitude

$$
\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^2}{mh}.
$$

One unit of emergent gauge charge has

$$
Q=\frac{e}{m},
\qquad
\theta=\frac{\pi}{m}.
$$

The microscopic polynomial, chiral edge theory, and bulk Chern–Simons action agree once they are known to describe the same gapped phase.

## Thought Striker — deliberately false

> Any lowest-Landau-level polynomial with the Laughlin pairwise zero automatically defines a gapped topological phase with charge-$e/m$ anyons.

The proposition compresses several different claims into one.

The pairwise zero exactly implies missing low-relative-angular-momentum channels and a parent-Hamiltonian zero mode. Flux counting identifies a densest allowed state.

But a robust topological phase additionally requires an isolated ground-state sector, a bulk gap, screening, and stability under local perturbations.

Some elegant clustering wavefunctions instead describe critical states. Algebraic beauty does not guarantee incompressibility.

The defensible statement is:

> Laughlin clustering supplies the microscopic zero-mode skeleton; a gap and screening promote that skeleton into topological order.

## Collision

The apparent disagreement is resolved by separating exact statements from phase-level inferences.

- **Exact algebra:** order-$m$ pair zeros eliminate pseudopotential channels with $r<m$.
- **Exact counting:** polynomial degree fixes the densest zero-mode filling and quasihole flux insertion.
- **Dynamical input:** a stable bulk gap and screening localize the defect and protect adiabatic transport.
- **Infrared description:** edge CFT and $U(1)_m$ Chern–Simons theory encode the resulting topological phase.

The local zero does not single-handedly prove global topology. It is the microscopic constraint from which global topology becomes reconstructible once the missing dynamical hypotheses are supplied.

## Exercise

Consider three fermions in the lowest Landau level with polynomial

$$
P(z_1,z_2,z_3)
=(z_1-z_2)^3(z_1-z_3)^3(z_2-z_3)^3.
$$

Fix $z_3$ and define

$$
R=\frac{z_1+z_2}{2},
\qquad
z=z_1-z_2.
$$

1. Show that $P\propto z^3$ as $z\to0$.
2. Explain why this implies $P_{12}^{(1)}P=0$.
3. Insert a quasihole, $P_{mathrm{qh}}(\eta)=\prod_{i=1}^{3}(z_i-\eta)P$. Distinguish the increase in total degree from the increase in magnetic flux.
4. State the additional physical assumption needed to infer charge $e/3$ from this defect.

</details>

Relative angular momentum in the lowest Landau level is the power of the relative coordinate. Magnetic flux is controlled by the maximum degree of one coordinate, not by total degree.

</details>

**Oral check 1.** Why does being the unique densest zero mode not by itself prove a thermodynamic gap?

**Oral check 2.** Where does plasma screening enter the argument for a localized fractional quasihole charge?

<details class="solution">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Using $z_1=R+z/2$ and $z_2=R-z/2$,

$$
z_1-z_2=z,
$$

while

$$
(z_1-z_3)^3(z_2-z_3)^3
=\left[(R-z_3)^2-\frac{z^2}{4}\right]^3.
$$

The second factor is finite and even in $z$ near coincidence. Hence

$$
P=z^3\left[(R-z_3)^2-\frac{z^2}{4}\right]^3
\sim z^3.
$$

The relative-coordinate expansion therefore begins at angular momentum $r=3$. There is no $r=1$ component, so

$$
P_{12}^{(1)}P=0.
$$

The quasihole factor adds degree one to each of the three coordinates. The total polynomial degree increases by $N=3$, while the maximum single-coordinate degree increases by one.

The latter is the flux count:

$$
\Delta N_{\phi}=1.
$$

For a gapped, screened $\nu=1/3$ Hall fluid, adiabatic insertion of one flux quantum creates a localized deficit with

$$
Q_{\mathrm{qh}}=\frac{e}{3}.
$$

Degree counting identifies the flux defect. The Hall response or plasma screening is the additional input that turns it into a localized fractional charge.

</details>
